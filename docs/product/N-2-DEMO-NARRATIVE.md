## The one idea

**Your Hermes is single-player. Herd makes it multiplayer.** Hermes already lets
*your* bots talk to each other (Group Chat). Herd puts **you and a teammate**,
each on your own machine with your own agent, in one shared channel. The story
is about two people, not two bots. See N-1 "Positioning vs. core Group Chat."

## Notes for Herald

- **Shot 4 changes slightly.** In v1 nobody's agent wakes up by itself
  (auto-wake is OUT, see N-1). Riley asks her agent to pick up the task. Say it
  out loud; it reads as intentional, not a limitation: *"She hands it to her
  agent."*
- **Pick a task that shows "her machine."** Best: something only her Mac has,
  e.g. "summarize the notes file on Riley's desktop" or "check what's on Riley's
  calendar Friday." Avoid tasks either agent could do alone, or the handoff
  looks pointless.
- **Agent labels must be visible** in every shot after beat 4. The viewer has
  to see which messages are humans and which are agents.
- **Hook pick:** change it. "This is one agent. This is another. They've never
  met." pitches agent-to-agent, which core Hermes already does. Use:
  **"Hermes is single-player. We made it multiplayer."** Alt for X: "My wife
  and I just put our Hermes agents in the same channel."
- **Shot 2 is now the Add-teammate form**, not `hermes peer add` in a terminal
  (Sentry's Q-3 ruling, see N-1 R-1).
- **Riley approves her own agent on camera** (one click is enough). That's the
  moment that shows Herd isn't Group Chat: her agent, her machine, her say.
- **Don't say:** "peer-to-peer," "gateway," "fan-out," "SQLite." Do say:
  "channel," "connect," "her agent," "his agent."
- **Fallback:** if live beat 4 flakes on record day, use Sentry's pre-recorded
  take of beat 4 only. Beats 2–3 must be live.
